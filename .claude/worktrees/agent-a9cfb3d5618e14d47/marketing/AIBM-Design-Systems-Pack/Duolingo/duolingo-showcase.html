Reverse-engineered design system

Design System
Analysis of Duolingo

A bright, candy-colored, game-like system: a snow-white canvas, soft "eel" grey ink, and one electric Feather Green that owns every call to action — pressed into chunky 3D buttons with a darker-green bottom lip.

02 — Foundations

Typography

Two proprietary faces — duolingo-sans (body / UI) and feather (display) — shown here with their nearest free substitutes, Nunito and Fredoka. Hierarchy is built from size and weight; note the unusually confident 500-weight body text.

04 — Components

Cards & Surfaces

Cards are flat white with a 1px Swan hairline and a 16px radius; they lift with a soft grey glow on hover rather than a heavy drop shadow.

06 — Foundations

Spacing Scale

A 4px base unit scaling to a 48px section rhythm.

07 — Foundations

Border Radius

Everything is rounded — 12px dominates buttons, inputs and cards; pills and avatars go fully round.

08 — Foundations

Elevation & Depth

Level 0 — flat surface separated by a 1px Swan hairline. The default.
Level 1 — the chunky solid offset (no blur) that forms the button lip and the brand's tactile, pressable feel.
Level 2 — a soft grey glow reserved for raised popovers and elevated cards.
09 — Responsive

Responsive Behavior

NameWidthKey changes
Mobile< 768pxSingle column; headlines drop toward ~40px; CTAs stretch to full width, keeping radius + green lip.
Tablet768–1023pxTwo-column course / feature grids; colour bands stay full-bleed.
Laptop1024–1279pxUp to four-column grids; content capped to a centered max width.
Desktop≥ 1280pxGenerous whitespace; oversized 64px hero; alternating white / pale-blue / indigo bands.

The personality lives in colour, roundness and weight — so the brand survives every breakpoint even as the layout simplifies to a single column.
